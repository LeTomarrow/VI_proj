Green Paradox

What the data shows

  1. Greener is not cleaner by itself. Renewables grew, but demand grew faster, so world CO₂e rose 81%.
  2. Europe and Asia went opposite ways. Europe's CO₂e fell by a third; Asia's more than tripled.
  3. Large renewable gains can still mean more CO₂e. Turkey, Jordan and China added renewables and still emit more.
  4. Why CO₂e changed differs by country. Portugal cleaned its mix; China made far more electricity.

Explore these yourself in the app. Data: Our World in Data, electricity only, 2000-2024; generation stands in for demand.

How the app was tested

Testers played Carla, an energy analyst writing a board briefing: has the growth of renewables reduced emissions from electricity, where, and why? As Carla, they did six tasks:

  1. T1 Which continent emits the most, and which cut CO₂e per kWh most?
  2. T2 What do Asia and Europe make their electricity from?
  3. T3 An Asian country that gained renewables but emits more.
  4. T4 European countries with dirty electricity and high use per person.
  5. T5 Three European countries side by side: which is cleanest?
  6. T6 Did its CO₂e change from making more electricity or a cleaner mix?

Heuristic evaluation

Both authors reviewed the app separately with Forsell & Johansson's ten heuristics for information visualisation, then merged the problems (severity 0-4).

User tests and SUS

Classmates, playing Carla, did tasks T1 to T6 and answered the System Usability Scale (0-100; 68 is the average). Answers are anonymous.
